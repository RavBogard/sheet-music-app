## How to work the sheet

Each block is one prayer. The rows that already name their arrangement come first — those are the
vocabulary you have been using. Then the rows that do not.

For each unnamed row, one of two answers: **an arrangement name**, or **"same as X"** if it is
another copy of an arrangement already listed. "Same as" is the more common answer and it is not a
failure — it is what turns a title into an identity.

Nothing needs doing in one sitting. Answer a block, or ten; the rest keeps.

## What happens with the answers

Each becomes an `edit_library_entry` call setting the row's title to carry its clarifier — the
same operator edit that repaired two titles in wave 2, one row at a time, each reversible. That is
what seeds `arrangement` for `L3` under R-0901-live-cw-1 §1.

Rows answered "same as X" are the more interesting half: they are the pairs that survived dedupe
because they are genuinely different files of the same arrangement — a lead sheet and a full
score, a scan and a re-engraving. **Those are not merged.** They keep both rows and share an
arrangement, which is exactly what the `arrangement` field is for and what a duplicate mark could
never express.
