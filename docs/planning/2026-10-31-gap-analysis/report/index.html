Where the weekly service flow guesses wrong, where the iPads lose the band's trust, and what to build next. Five researchers read the code and docs, walked production at iPad and desktop size, and called the live MCP. Your two rounds of answers shape the plan below.

What hurts most

Six headlines

S1 · weekly flow

"Usual" isn't usual

A chart counts as usual if it was played three or more times ever. Next Shabbat morning's draft fills the Moshav Mi Chamocha although 26 Sep used Ana B'Koach. Profiles were copied once from a Bat Mitzvah and a Camp Sabra service and never re-learn.

S1 · weekly flow

You say yes to rows you never heard

Every filled row is folded into "everything else is your usual". The readback never names the chart or key it filled, never compares with last week, and never warns that a setlist already exists for that date.

MCP-04 · MCP-05 · MCP-08 · MCP-11
S2 · wrong chart version

Versions become siblings

A v2 upload trips the duplicate check, so the agent forces it in as a separate chart. A real revision then re-points every past setlist and overwrites the keys they were played in. Two different charts are both named "Shema (major).pdf".

MCP-06 · MCP-10 · MCP-19 · WEB-03
S1 · Perform trust

Offline reload, stale lists, no page turns

Reloading offline with a chart open likely shows Safari's "no internet" page, and the test that claims otherwise doesn't really go offline. Signed-out iPads never notice setlist edits. Pages don't turn on a tap, and nothing says when the setlist was last updated.

PERF-01 · PERF-06 · PERF-05 · PERF-10
S1 · wedges

Nothing caps a wedge

A double-tap jumps a fader to 0 dB, and Claude can set a send to +10 dB, so a wedge can ring with feedback. No mix is ever saved, so a desk reset wipes everyone's levels. Bridge alerts reach admins but not Drew or Michael.

MIXER-03 · MIXER-01 · MIXER-11
S2 · second tenant

Brothers Lazaroff wears CRC's clothes

Their gig packets print "CRC Music Books", there is no venue, set-length or load-in field, draft_service is dead for band tenants, and the calendar still shows parashot.

Big bets, in the order you set

  1. Service memory rebuild

    Slots keyed by prayer moment, re-learned every week from regular services only, and a readback that names stale or risky rows and compares with last service. B'nei Mitzvah gets its own preset. Re-seeds the three profiles created during research. M–L

  2. Chart lineage

    A new upload can be a new version of a chart, not a sibling. Memory follows the newest version and past setlists keep their exact chart. Adds a chart chip in the editor and a song page with grouped variants. M–L

  3. Perform trust pack

    Edge tap zones (with a per-device off switch), the note, Vocal Lead and "Next: title (key)" over the open chart, the freshness line, a preflight that names failed charts, and nightly WebKit CI. M

  4. Wedge safety and memory

    Ceilings set by Drew and enforced in the bridge, saved mixes per musician and service type, and an engineer console showing all five buses. Needs a bridge release. M–L

  5. Brothers Lazaroff band mode

    A gig block (venue, times, set lengths with a running total), a draft from a named past gig, a Brothers Lazaroff agent guide, segues and per-singer keys. L

  6. Family spine

    An advisory week key on every setlist, emitted in today.json, so the setlist, Haftarah and Overlays join on one week. A Beit Mitzvah card on its setlist. M–L

By role

Who feels it

Daniel (author, via Claude)

  • Confidently wrong drafts: a stale usual, profiles seeded from a Bat Mitzvah or a camp service.
  • No "vs last week" in the readback, and file names spoken aloud.
  • Mixer: "show me my mix" fails because Claude's account owns no bus.
  • Three tools describe the weekly flow three different ways.

Band musicians (iPads)

  • Trust: no "last updated" and no per-chart cached mark.
  • Notes and "what's next" vanish once a chart opens.
  • No tap or pedal page turns, no night mode and no pencil marks, which are paper's strengths.
  • Bb/Eb transposition is forgotten every week when signed out.

David (2nd band leader)

  • Builds 4–7 instrument copies per service by hand.
  • Stale usuals are hard to replace in one sentence.
  • Bus 2 is the first bus only; a co-owned bus is changed silently.

Randy (leads some weeks)

  • Shabbat morning with Randy returns no_profile, although a "Randy Shabbat morning" template exists.
  • Recovery costs 3–4 calls because cloning drops the rabbi slot.

Drew (sound)

  • No ceilings to set, so feedback risk sits with whoever moves a fader.
  • Can't ride another musician's bus from the iPad.
  • Not alerted when the bridge is down, and his access request has been pending a month.

Michael (studio PC)

  • Not alerted when the bridge is down.
  • The watchdog install is unconfirmed, and the bridge's last error is blank.

Brothers Lazaroff

  • No show sheet: no venue, load-in or set lengths.
  • CRC wording on packets and footers, and parashot in the calendar.
  • No weekly draft flow; cloning is the only route.
