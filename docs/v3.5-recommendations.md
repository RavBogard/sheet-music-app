# CentralReform v3.5 — Feature Recommendations

Four proposals based on the v3.1 codebase audit. Each builds on infrastructure that already exists.

---

## 1. Offline-First Library with Background Sync

**The problem:** Musicians open the app during services or rehearsals and hit a loading spinner. The library requires a network fetch on every cold start — all ~180 files from Firestore through the API. If the synagogue wifi hiccups mid-service, the app becomes a blank screen.

**The fix:** Cache the entire library index in IndexedDB via the existing service worker. On first load, the library downloads and caches. On subsequent loads, it reads from cache instantly and syncs changes in the background. PDFs that a musician has viewed get cached too — so their sheet music works offline.

**Why it works now:** The library store already loads everything in a single `?all=true` request (optimized in v3.1). The Fuse.js index is already built client-side. The PWA service worker is registered. This is connecting existing pieces.

**What ships:**
- `IndexedDB` cache layer under `useLibraryStore` — stores `allFiles` with a version hash
- Background delta sync: on load, serve cached data immediately, then check Firestore `lastSyncedAt` timestamp against cached version, fetch only if stale
- PDF pre-caching: when a musician opens a setlist, pre-fetch all the PDFs in the background so they're ready during the service
- Annotations and transposition preferences already live in Firestore per-user — these sync naturally when connectivity returns

**Impact:** App loads in <100ms instead of 1-3 seconds. Works in airplane mode. No more service disruptions from wifi drops.

## 2. Liturgical Smart Setlists

**The problem:** Every week, the music director builds a setlist for Shabbat services by manually adding songs in roughly the same liturgical order — Candle Lighting, Shema, Mi Chamocha, Torah service, etc. — swapping in seasonal variations and the correct Torah portion. It's the same structure with small changes, repeated 52 times a year plus holidays.

**The fix:** A template engine that knows the Reform liturgical structure and the Jewish calendar. The AI chat or a dedicated UI can generate a complete setlist from a single prompt: "Shabbat morning for February 21" → pre-populated with the congregation's standard sequence, correct Torah portion (Ki Tisa), and seasonal pieces. The music director reviews and tweaks rather than building from scratch.

**Why it works now:** The `Setlist` type already has `isTemplate` and `templateType` fields (defined but unused). The AI chat agent already has `CREATE_SETLIST` and `ADD_TO_SETLIST` commands. The library has enriched metadata (topics, keys) from the Gemini enrichment engine. The infrastructure is there — it just needs the liturgical calendar data and template definitions.

**What ships:**
- `lib/liturgical-calendar.ts` — Hebrew date utilities, parasha lookup, holiday detection (uses Hebcal API or static table)
- Template definitions: `shabbat_morning`, `friday_night`, `high_holidays`, `festival` — each defines the liturgical sequence with slots that map to library search queries
- "New Setlist from Template" button in the setlist dashboard
- AI chat enhancement: "Build me a setlist for this Friday" → detects date, identifies it's a regular Shabbat, applies the friday_night template, searches library for matches, generates the setlist
- The music director gets a 90% complete setlist in seconds

**Impact:** Saves 15-20 minutes per week of manual setlist building. Ensures nothing gets forgotten in the liturgical order. Makes it easy for guest music directors to build proper setlists without institutional knowledge.

## 3. Rehearsal Mode

**The problem:** Musicians practice by opening a chart in performance mode and playing along with a recording on a separate app (Spotify, YouTube, voice memo). There's no way to slow down a tricky section, loop the bridge, or sync the chart to the audio. The audio infrastructure exists in the app (`audioFileId` on tracks, the `/audio` page) but it's disconnected from the performance view.

**The fix:** A dedicated rehearsal view that syncs audio playback with chart display. Tempo control lets you slow down to 50% with pitch correction. Tap-to-loop lets you select a section and repeat it. A simple recording mode captures your instrument alongside the backing track.

**Why it works now:** `SetlistTrack` already has `audioFileId` and `bpm` fields. The music store already manages audio state (`audio.fileId`, `audio.isPlaying`, `audio.volume`, `audio.isLooping`). The Tone.js library is available for time-stretching. The performance view has keyboard shortcuts. This is wiring together things that already exist in isolation.

**What ships:**
- Rehearsal toolbar in performance mode: appears when a track has an `audioFileId`
- Tempo slider: 0.5x → 1.5x using Tone.js `GrainPlayer` for pitch-corrected time stretching
- Loop markers: tap start point and end point on a timeline, section repeats
- Auto-scroll sync: if `bpm` is set, the chart scrolls at the tempo of the audio
- Record-along: MediaRecorder captures mic input, plays back with the backing track so you can hear yourself
- Practice stats: time spent per song (tracked in Firestore user preferences)

**Impact:** Musicians actually practice with the app instead of just referencing charts at the last minute. Better-prepared ensemble. The recording feature lets people share practice takes with the music director for feedback.

## 4. Musician Practice Dashboard

**The problem:** The current homepage is a greeting + upcoming setlists + quick search. It's passive — it tells you what's coming but not what you need to do. Musicians open the app, glance at the setlist, close it. There's no sense of "here's your prep for this week" or "this song changed since you last looked at it."

**The fix:** A personalized practice dashboard that turns the app from a reference tool into a daily practice companion. It shows what's different since your last visit, what needs your attention, and tracks your preparation progress.

**Why it works now:** All the data already exists in Firestore:
- `users/{uid}/songPreferences` — tracks which songs you've interacted with
- `users/{uid}/annotations` — tracks which charts you've marked up
- `setlists` with `eventDate` — upcoming gigs
- `library_index` with `lastSyncedAt` — when files were updated
- `setlists/{id}/presence` — who's been looking at the setlist

**What ships:**
- "Your Week" section: upcoming setlists you're involved in, with preparation checklist (viewed chart? set transposition? annotated?)
- "What's New" section: songs in your upcoming setlists that were updated since you last viewed them (new arrangement, key change, updated chart)
- Preparation progress: a simple progress ring per setlist — "4 of 7 charts reviewed" — visible to the music director in the admin dashboard
- Quick practice launcher: one tap to open your next setlist in rehearsal mode, starting from where you left off
- Optional gentle nudges: "Shabbat is in 2 days — you haven't looked at Mi Chamocha yet" (configurable, not annoying)

**Impact:** Musicians come to rehearsal and services better prepared. Music directors can see at a glance who's looked at the charts. Creates a positive habit loop — open the app daily instead of scrambling the morning of services.

## Recommended Priority

| Feature | Effort | Musician Value | Music Director Value |
|---------|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| 1. Offline-First | Medium | Very High | Medium |
| 2. Liturgical Smart Setlists | Medium | Low | Very High |
| 3. Rehearsal Mode | Large | Very High | High |
| 4. Practice Dashboard | Medium | High | Very High |

**If I could only pick two:** Offline-First (#1) and Liturgical Smart Setlists (#2). They're both medium effort, and together they transform the app from "useful tool" to "indispensable infrastructure." #1 makes it reliable, #2 makes it save the music director real time every week.

**If you want the biggest splash:** Rehearsal Mode (#3) is the feature that makes musicians actually *want* to open the app between services. It's larger in scope but has the highest engagement potential.
