## CRC — Central Reform Congregation

> **RATIFIED DIRECTION (Daniel, 2026-06-11): the mural IS the brand.** CRC is
> undertaking a broad branding makeover, moving AWAY from the hands logo and
> the site's plum (#7A356E) and TOWARD the Siona mural — its colors and vibe.
> The app leads this. Treat centralreform.org's current plum/Playfair styling
> as LEGACY, not a target; the design-review recommendation to re-hue toward
> plum is overridden. One direction, mural-led: cobalt/ultramarine primary,
> crimson and golden-amber warmth, jewel-tone accents (see observed palette
> below). The hands logo remains a real asset (it still appears in places)
> but is no longer the identity anchor.

### Who they say they are (centralreform.org, verbatim fragments)
"A vibrant urban Jewish community dedicated to supporting one another in holy
ways, pursuing justice and lifelong learning, and providing a shelter of
peace." · "A Jewish Presence in the City of St. Louis." Services are
"welcoming, egalitarian, flexible, and in both English and Hebrew."

### Identity signals
- **Voice:** warm, plain-spoken, inclusive; sacred vocabulary worn lightly
  ("holy ways", "shelter of peace", "shalom@"). Notably the wordmark is
  styled **lowercase** — "central reform congregation" — humble, human.
- **Justice-forward:** "Justice June", advocacy pages, inclusive-progress-flag
  heart in the footer; support groups (grief, recovery), The Nest. This is a
  community of *care* — the brand is people, not architecture.
- **Visual assets:** the **Siona floor mural** in the oneg room — per Daniel
  (2026-06-11): **"CRC's true brand these days IS the Siona mural."** It is
  the primary visual source of truth, ahead of the hands logo (filename
  `newcolorCRClogo`, multicolor — still worth sampling, but secondary).
- **Imagery on the site:** people in community — Torah study on Zoom, event
  photos, hand-drawn event art. Warmth over polish.

### The mural, observed (Claude viewed `Siona.Floor.png` 2026-06-11)
A circular mandala in Siona Benjamin's Indian-miniature-meets-Jewish style:
concentric rings — a **golden amber core** (the twelve tribes, named in Hebrew
+ English), a **jewel-tone segmented ring of the Hebrew months** (teal, coral,
marigold, sage, lavender), an **ultramarine/cobalt outer field** populated by
her signature blue-skinned figures, lions, trees, rainbow, star — all bound by
a **deep crimson border**.

**Observed palette for the accent system:** deep cobalt/ultramarine (dominant)
· crimson red (border/emphasis) · golden amber (warm heart) · jewel accents
(teal, coral, marigold, sage, lavender) · the sky-blue figure tone.

**Daniel (2026-06-11): teal and lavender are CORE colors, not accents** —
the working core is cobalt + teal + lavender, with crimson emphasis and amber
warmth.

**RATIFIED v2 (Daniel, 2026-06-11): NO SOLO COLOR — the brand is the wheel.**
v3 palette felt "too blue"; cobalt is not the main color. CRC's identity is
the mural's POLYCHROME ensemble — the full jewel spectrum, structured the way
the mural structures it: rings/segments, a gold-amber warm heart, a crimson
frame, calm grounding so the colors glow rather than shout. "Everything people
encounter with CRC should remind them of the mural." This is a full brand
redefinition (beyond the app). Working hypothesis for the signature device:
a segmented-ring motif abstracted from the month ring.

**ANTI-CHURCHY RULE (Daniel, 2026-06-11, after brand-moment round 1):** the
mural is joyful Indian-miniature folk art, NOT liturgical solemnity. Never:
heavy display serifs at large sizes, bronze/ochre gravitas, stained-glass
depth, symmetric cathedral compositions. Always: bright saturation, generous
light space, playfulness. Stylization comes from vibrancy + ring geometry,
not gravitas. (Round-1 verdict: B's voice + A's ambition.)

**PERSONALITY (Daniel, 2026-06-11, after round C):** Jewish in a
Middle-Eastern-flavored way, with the South Asian touch present in Siona's
art. NEVER techy-bro/geometric-modern/startup-clean (round-C failure: Outfit
as display). Voice lives in letterforms with calligraphic/Hebrew/Persian DNA
and in ornament abstracted from the mural's patterning — flavor through type
DNA, not pastiche. Bilingual (Latin + Hebrew) lockups are native to CRC.

**Priority (Daniel, 2026-06-11): CRC's brand leads; BL is very secondary.**
Design CRC unconstrained. If the tenants end up reading too similar (both
teal-adjacent), BL adjusts around CRC later — never the reverse. Tenant
distinguishability remains a *check* (the apps must not be confusable on
shared iPads), but it is BL's problem to solve, not a constraint on CRC.

**Two design gifts in the composition itself:**
1. The current app hue (275 violet) is actually NEAR the mural's cobalt
   (~262) — the failure isn't hue, it's that the app drained all the chroma
   and has none of the crimson/amber warmth. The fix may be saturation +
   warm accents, not a hue revolution. Continuity, deepened.
2. The mural IS a calendar — months and tribes in a wheel. An app whose
   heartbeat is the weekly Shabbat cycle has a ready-made brand story:
   the wheel of the year. (Month-segment colors could even key seasonal
   accents — an idea for the reviewer to test, not a commitment.)

### The gap (current app)
The CRC app theme is a single cool hue — **oklch hue 275 (violet-gray)
across every token** — clean and legible but expressing nothing above. This
is the "too utilitarian" Daniel named. There is no CRC imagery, no warmth, no
trace of the hands, the mural, or the lowercase humility.

### Direction hypotheses (for the review to test, not conclusions)
1. **The mural IS the palette.** Build the CRC accent system by sampling the
   Siona mural directly — a high-resolution photo of it is the foundational
   design asset (Daniel can supply one if the web versions are too small).
   Keep a calm light canvas (it's a Friday-evening reading surface); the
   mural's colors arrive as accents, gradients, and moments — warmth and joy
   against quiet ground, candle-glow not corporate.
2. **Mural as identity anchor, not decoration:** login/landing hero treatment
   (the BL Joe's-Cafe equivalent), empty states, email header art — the
   places people pause. Perform mode stays quiet.
3. **"Shelter of peace" as a design brief:** soft edges, generous whitespace,
   gentle elevation — the app should feel like being welcomed in.
4. **Lowercase wordmark treatment** in the app header, mirroring the site.
5. **Hebrew-aware typography:** titles are bilingual liturgy — choose/verify a
   type pairing that sets Hebrew names with dignity, not fallback-font shrug.

## Brothers Lazaroff

### Who they say they are (brotherslazaroff.com)
"An eclectic Saint Louis-based band… seamless fusion of folk, rock, blues, and
subtle jazz." Press swagger is core brand: "The most eclectic and versatile
band in town" (Steve Pick) · "hard to label except as pure Brothers Lazaroff"
(Alive). Franchises: **Hanukkah Hullabaloo** (13 annual!), **LazJazzFest**,
**Laz Jazz Artist Lounge @ MATI**, **Saturday Sessions at Tower Grove
Farmers' Market**.

### Identity signals
- **Wordmark:** white-and-blue Western-slab with light shadow (already in the
  app since v11.1). ALL-CAPS nav voice on the site.
- **Palette in production app (v11.1):** canvas oklch(0.16 .028 198) ≈ their
  #002020 deep teal; accent oklch(0.72 .115 210) ≈ #43B7CE cyan. **Zilla
  Slab** headings. Joe's Cafe hero photo on login.
- **Photography is the brand:** professional live shots throughout
  (credited: Vertrell Yates, Phillip Hamer, Annie Martineau) — stage light,
  sweat, brass, crowd. Plus dedicated **texture/divider art assets**
  (`Brothers+Lazaroff-Background.png`, divider PNGs) — they already think in
  poster terms.
- **Band-member culture:** individual players showcased with handles — the
  collective is part of the show.

### The gap (current app)
v11.1 landed wordmark + palette + one hero. "More swagger" (Daniel) =
the identity is present but thin: one photo, no texture, conservative type
scale, no poster energy in setlist/show surfaces.

### Direction hypotheses
1. **Texture + photography depth:** pull the site's background/divider art
   into surface backdrops; rotate live photography (rights are in-family —
   note photographer credits convention).
2. **Gig-poster typography:** let Zilla Slab get LOUD on landing/show
   headers — sizes and weight contrast a poster would use; keep Perform mode
   itself quiet (legibility is sacred there).
3. **Show-first framing:** broslaz setlists ARE shows ("Tower Grove Farmer's
   Market") — date/venue treatment styled like the site's SHOWS block.
4. **Franchise moments:** Hullabaloo/LazJazz events could get accent art
   without new code (imagery slots).
